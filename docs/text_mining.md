# Text mining: sentiment and topics (SCRUM-28)

Generated by `python3 src/text_mining.py` from `data/processed/text_corpus.csv` (SCRUM-23). Do not hand-edit; re-running with the same inputs gives the same CSV files.

**Descriptive only.** These outputs describe what the comments say per product, cohort and brand, for the review and the dashboard. They are not model features: the leak-free daily inputs for modelling are SCRUM-24's `processed/method2_*` files (`src/isolate_method2_data.py`).

## Outputs

- `data/processed/text_sentiment_comments.csv`: one row per corpus row (29,189): VADER score and label (missing for flagged rows, with `sentiment_skip_reason`), `vader_no_sentiment_words`, aspect mentions, LDA topic shares and dominant topic. Join to the text on `(product_id, comment_id)`.
- `data/processed/text_sentiment_by_product.csv`: sentiment per registry product (60 rows).

## Sentiment

- Tool: VADER (`vaderSentiment`) compound score on `text_sentiment_ready`, which keeps negations, punctuation and emojis (VADER uses all three). Labels: positive if compound >= 0.05, negative if <= -0.05, else neutral.
- Scored: usable comments only (`is_usable`: none of emoji-only, likely non-English, empty after cleaning, too short). Flagged rows stay in the output with the score **missing, not 0**.
- Per product: `mean_compound`, label shares, and `like_weighted_mean_compound`, which weights each comment by 1 + its likes (so a comment with no likes still counts once).
- Cohort and brand rows count a comment shared by several products of the group once.

Decisions:
- Sentiment words (good, best, love, great, nice) are **kept**: they appear in a few topics but do not take them over, and praise is itself a theme of these comments.
- Brand and model words are **removed from the topic vocabulary** (they stay in `text_clean`). Kept, they made up 50.0% of LDA's top terms and 60.0% of NMF's at the same k, and the topics became brands (for example: pro iphone ultra 15 vivo). The product a comment belongs to already says its brand, so brand topics add nothing; without them the topics are about what people discuss (coherence 0.021 vs 0.093 with brands kept; brand topics are coherent but uninformative).

Models: LDA (sklearn, batch, 20 iterations) on word counts and NMF on TF-IDF, k = 4 to 10, random_state 42. Scores per fit: coherence (mean NPMI of each topic's top 10 terms, from document co-occurrence; higher is better, 0 means the terms co-occur as often as chance), stability (refit on 2 random 80% subsamples, mean best-match Jaccard of top-10 terms; 1 means identical topics) and overlap (mean Jaccard between topics; lower is more distinct). The k with the best average rank of coherence and stability is chosen per method (ties: smaller k). The reported topics are LDA's; NMF is the comparison.

| method | k | coherence | stability | overlap | chosen |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|:-:|
| LDA | 3 | 0.021 | 0.266 | 0.018 | yes |
| NMF | 3 | 0.085 | 0.777 | 0.113 | yes |

Chosen: **LDA k=3** (NMF's best: k=3). Read the terms below before trusting the choice: the scores rank fits, they do not prove the topics are meaningful.

Honest reading: on comments this short (median 5 tokens) all fits score low. LDA's chosen fit has coherence 0.021 and stability 0.266: only part of each topic's top terms comes back on a subsample. NMF's is more stable (0.777, coherence 0.085) but its topics share more terms (overlap 0.113 vs 0.018). ## Aspects

## Limitations

- **VADER is English-only.** Hinglish, other romanised Indian languages and Latin-script languages such as Indonesian pass the `likely_non_english` check (it judges the script, not the language) and are scored as English: most of their words are not in VADER's lexicon (bahut, acha, bekar, mast are not), so they come out neutral. 29.5% of scored comments have compound exactly 0 (no sentiment word found); read the neutral share as "no English sentiment detected", not "neutral opinion". About 186 scored comments contain 2+ common Indonesian words (a rough count); they form their own topic above.
- VADER was built for social media English; sarcasm, comparisons ("the S25 is better than this") and phone jargon are misread ("heating", "drain", "overpriced", "hang" are not in its lexicon; "lag" is). The spot-check file exists to measure this; no accuracy figure is claimed until it is labelled.
- Generic words dominate the comments ("phone" is in 16% of them, "video" in 9%): without the domain stop-list the topics are about YouTube and brands, not phones. The stop-list is a judgement call, listed above with frequencies so it can be challenged.
- **Shared comments.** 5,178 corpus rows are comments shared by 2+ products (one video in several products' search results). Per-product numbers include them for every product they belong to; cohort and brand numbers count them once; the topic models see each once.
- The sample is narrow: top-level comments from each product's 5 most-viewed sampled videos, at most 100 per video, in relevance order. It describes those videos' audiences, not all buyers.
- Topic labels are keyword-based suggestions; confirm them by reading the terms and a few comments.

## ⚠️ Leakage: descriptive only

**The comments were collected on 2026-10-03/04** as a relevance-ranked sample of the videos most viewed by then. Every number here is a snapshot of that October sample. **Do not use these text outputs as time-series predictors for earlier dates** (a past day only has the comments that survived into the October sample). For modelling, use SCRUM-24's leak-free `processed/method2_train.csv` and `method2_test.csv`, whose text features are lagged and fitted on training-period comments only, and keep its caveats.

## Spot check: VADER vs an AI review (not human labels)

**Read this first:** the reference labels (`ai_label` in `data/processed/text_sentiment_spotcheck.csv`) come from an AI review that read each comment's text, without seeing VADER's label; they are **not human annotations**, and `label_source` says so on every row. `human_label` is left empty for anyone who wants to add real labels. The numbers below are agreement between VADER and that AI reader, not VADER's accuracy against human judgement.

Rules used by the review: positive or negative for a clear tone about the phone, brand, review or reviewer; neutral for questions, requests and facts; the dominant tone for mixed comments; unclear for comments it could not read reliably and off-topic ones (greetings, jokes, spam).
